## Methodology

This paper is written as a perspective-style review rather than an experimental report. Its method is therefore comparative and source-driven: it evaluates recent quantum-computing claims by separating hardware scale, logical-qubit behavior, and systems-level fault-tolerance assumptions. The unit of analysis is not the qubit count alone, but the combination of physical-qubit scale, code distance, logical error suppression, decoder latency, and whether a result was actually demonstrated on hardware or only projected in a roadmap.

The comparison starts by grouping evidence into three platform families: superconducting, trapped-ion, and neutral-atom systems. Within each family, the paper prioritizes primary-source milestones with concrete dates and measured figures. For superconducting systems, this includes Google’s December 9, 2024 Willow result and its January 13, 2026 dynamic surface-code follow-up. For trapped-ion systems, the paper distinguishes Quantinuum’s April 3, 2024 logical-qubit announcement from its September 10, 2024 roadmap. For neutral atoms, it treats the December 6, 2023 Nature paper on reconfigurable atom arrays and the November 10, 2025 fault-tolerant architecture paper as evidence of platform breadth, not as proof that one approach has solved the full scaling problem.

The paper then applies an explicit demonstrated-versus-roadmap filter. Demonstrated claims are limited to measurements reported on hardware, such as threshold behavior, logical qubit lifetime, logical error-rate suppression, or repeated error-correction cycles. Roadmap claims are treated separately, including IBM’s June 10, 2025 large-scale fault-tolerance plan and Quantinuum’s 2030 universal fault-tolerant roadmap [ibm2025_large] [quantinuum2024_roadmap]. Where a source mixes both, the paper parses them into separate sentences so that a current experimental milestone is not mistaken for a future target.

This approach is intentionally conservative. It does not rank platforms as winners or assume a single architectural path will dominate. Instead, it asks whether each system is moving toward logical computation that improves with scale, and whether the evidence supports a claim about protected computation rather than a claim about raw device size. That distinction is the central methodological constraint of the review.
