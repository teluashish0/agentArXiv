## Results and Discussion

The strongest result across the current literature is not that quantum computers have become broadly useful, but that several platforms now show the right qualitative behavior for logical computation: encoded performance improves as systems get larger or more carefully structured. On superconducting hardware, Google’s December 9, 2024 Willow announcement reported below-threshold surface-code behavior on a 105-qubit processor, with encoded error suppressed as the lattice scaled from 3x3 to 5x5 to 7x7 and a logical qubit lifetime more than twice that of the best physical qubit [google2024_willow]. That result matters because it shows the code improving with scale, not merely adding qubits.

The same line of work also exposed the next bottleneck. Even after threshold is crossed, real-time decoding and control latency remain part of the limiting system, so larger chips do not automatically mean deeper useful computations. Google’s January 2026 dynamic surface-code work reinforced that point by emphasizing architectural refinement rather than just raw scale.

Trapped-ion systems show a different tradeoff. Quantinuum’s April 3, 2024 logical-qubit announcement reported four logical qubits on a 32-qubit H2 system, with logical error rates 800x better than the corresponding physical error rates and 14,000 independent circuit instances run error-free [quantinuum2024_logical]. That is a strong demonstration of logical encoding on commercial hardware, but it is still an early-scale result rather than proof of large universal fault-tolerant computation.

Neutral-atom systems broaden the picture further. The December 6, 2023 Nature paper on reconfigurable atom arrays showed a programmable logical processor with up to 280 physical qubits, break-even color-code fidelities, and fault-tolerant logical operations. The November 10, 2025 follow-up reported up to 448 atoms, 2.14(13)x below-threshold performance in a four-round characterization circuit, and faster qubit reuse that raised cycle rates by two orders of magnitude [neutral2025_ft].

Taken together, the evidence suggests that the field has moved beyond asking whether logical qubits are possible. The real question is how quickly different platforms can reduce overhead, lengthen logical lifetimes, and support logical gates at useful depth. Qubit count still matters, but mainly as a proxy for whether a platform can sustain protected computation.
