Distributed quantum computing has long been proposed as a scaling strategy for architectures that are easier to control in modules than as one monolithic processor. In that framing, a networked system combines two layers: locally well-controlled circuit qubits and remote links that distribute entanglement between network qubits. Earlier literature often treated the remote layer as a theoretical primitive, usually through gate teleportation or entanglement swapping, while leaving the engineering question of rate, fidelity, and repeated operation unresolved. What has changed in the latest work is not that the theory became different, but that several of those assumptions are now being tested on hardware [main2025_dqc] [lee2026_photonic_vqe].

The recent literature falls into three categories. First are link-layer experiments, which measure whether remote entanglement between memory-bearing nodes can be generated with enough fidelity and repetition rate to matter for modular computation. The time-bin trapped-ion result in Nature Communications and multiplexed multi-emitter network-node work in Nature both sit in this category: they do not by themselves prove distributed quantum computing, but they improve the credibility of the network substrate it would require [hucul2025_timebin] [ruskuc2025_nodes]. Second are distributed-computation demonstrations, where the network is used to execute a remote gate or an algorithm rather than only to produce a Bell pair. The Oxford trapped-ion Nature paper and the 2026 photonic distributed VQE paper represent this stronger class of evidence because they connect the interconnect layer to actual computation [main2025_dqc] [lee2026_photonic_vqe].

The third category is architecture-level positioning from vendors and roadmap documents. IBM describes networking as a way to scale beyond the limits of a single processor package, IonQ frames modular photonic interconnects as part of its long-term trapped-ion scaling plan, and PsiQuantum treats optical interconnect as native to its silicon-photonics approach [ibm2025_networked] [ionq2025_aria] [ionq2025_lightsynq] [psiquantum2025_omega]. These sources are useful because they show where platform builders think the bottlenecks are moving. They are weaker, however, than hardware demonstrations, and this paper keeps that distinction explicit throughout.
