The strongest recent evidence comes from experiments that move beyond remote entanglement as an isolated feat and tie it directly to distributed computation. In the February 2025 Nature paper on distributed quantum computing across an optical network link, two trapped-ion modules separated by about 2 meters executed teleported CZ interactions, composed distributed iSWAP and SWAP operations, and ran Grover's algorithm on the combined machine [main2025_dqc]. The headline is not that the system was large. It was small. The important point is that the network supported more than one non-local operation and that the local memory qubits remained useful while new rounds of entanglement were generated. That is qualitatively different from a single Bell-pair demonstration.

Link-layer experiments still matter because they determine whether such distributed gates can ever become routine rather than heroic. The 2025 Nature Communications trapped-ion result reported Bell-state fidelity of 0.970(4) for remote entanglement via time-bin photons, an average entanglement rate of 0.35 per second, and a path the authors argue could reach fidelity above 0.999 with higher rates under improved operating conditions [hucul2025_timebin]. Multiplexed and multi-emitter network-node demonstrations add a second systems point: scalable distributed computing will require not only high fidelity but also bandwidth, memory coordination, and parallelism across many entanglement opportunities [ruskuc2025_nodes] [metz2025_metropolitan]. These results do not yet amount to large distributed computers, but they materially improve the plausibility of the interconnect layer.

Photonic distributed computing broadens the picture. The January 3, 2026 npj Quantum Information paper demonstrated a two-qubit VQE across two spatially separated single-photon processors connected through 3 kilometers of optical fiber, using pre-shared entanglement and weak measurements to access the full Hilbert space without conventional non-local gate overhead [lee2026_photonic_vqe]. This is still a small-scale algorithmic result, but it matters because it shows a network doing computation rather than merely moving states around.

Commercial and vendor-facing sources suggest that architecture teams now treat networking as central to scaling, but they remain weaker evidence than the experiments above. IBM's April 2025 roadmap update and its May 2025 networked-quantum-computers blog frame modular processors and quantum links as part of the path beyond single-package scale [ibm2025_roadmap] [ibm2025_networked]. IonQ's January 2025 architectural write-up ties trapped-ion scaling to QCCD plus photonic interconnects, and its May 7, 2025 Lightsynq announcement makes the networking layer explicit as a route to larger modular systems [ionq2025_aria] [ionq2025_lightsynq]. PsiQuantum's Omega platform likewise treats optical interconnect as a native systems feature rather than an external afterthought [psiquantum2025_omega]. The defensible conclusion is therefore mixed: networking is now a credible scaling direction with real hardware milestones, but the decisive bottlenecks remain rate, repeatability, memory quality, and control overhead rather than visionary architecture slides.
