This review does not report a new laboratory experiment. Its "setup" is the evaluation lens used to compare recent networked-quantum-computing evidence. The paper focuses on trapped-ion and photonic results because those platforms currently provide the clearest experimental record for photonic interconnects, while IBM, IonQ, and PsiQuantum are treated as architecture and commercial signals about where scaling assumptions are moving [main2025_dqc] [lee2026_photonic_vqe] [ibm2025_networked] [psiquantum2025_omega].

The main variables of interest are remote entanglement fidelity, entanglement-generation rate, non-local gate support, and algorithmic depth. A link demonstration is stronger when it can generate entanglement repeatedly without forcing the local memory qubits to idle beyond useful coherence windows. A distributed-computation result is stronger when it does more than one remote interaction or supports a recognizable algorithm instead of a single proof-of-principle gate. The review also tracks whether a paper reports actual fiber distance, multiplexing, or memory-node integration, because those details matter for judging whether a link can ever move from lab optics to system architecture [hucul2025_timebin] [ruskuc2025_nodes].

Roadmap sources are evaluated differently. For IBM, IonQ, and PsiQuantum, the question is not whether the company has already demonstrated distributed fault tolerance, but whether networking is treated as a first-class scaling primitive and whether any of the required interconnect properties are already measured on hardware [ibm2025_roadmap] [ionq2025_aria] [ionq2025_lightsynq] [psiquantum2025_omega]. That separation keeps the paper realistic: architecture intent matters, but it is not the same thing as demonstrated distributed computation.
